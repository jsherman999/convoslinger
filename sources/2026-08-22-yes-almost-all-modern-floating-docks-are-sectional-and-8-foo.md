Yes — almost all modern floating docks are sectional, and 8-foot is one of the standard section lengths. There are two main approaches:

**Poly float modules** (EZ Dock, Wave Armor, PolyDock) — one-piece rotomolded HDPE sections joined by couplers. EZ Dock's coupler design flexes with waves, and because the system is modular you can lay out whatever shape you want or add sections later. Priciest per foot: a 3'×10' PolyDock section starts around $1,400 and a 5'×10' around $2,400, and dealer pricing for floating modules generally runs $1,600–$2,900+ each before install or anchoring.

**Frame-and-float sections** — aluminum or wood frame with poly floats bolted underneath, connected end to end. This is where 8-foot sections are common: a 4×8 floating truss section runs roughly $1,850 with cedar decking up to $2,050 with PVC, with 6×8 versions $2,050–$2,350, hardware included.

**DIY kits** are the cheap route. Menards carries PlayStar/Port Side — their 4'×10' wood dock frame kit includes all brackets and hardware and can be built as stationary, roll-in, or floating, though lumber isn't included. Fleet Farm and Home Depot carry Tommy Docks, PlayStar, and Patriot Docks. Figure $400–$900 per section once you add floats and lumber.

Two things worth knowing before you buy:

- **Sections aren't cross-brand compatible.** Couplers and connector brackets are proprietary, so pick a system first and add to it later rather than mixing an EZ Dock section with a Wave Armor one.
- **Watch the "all-in" question.** Some quotes exclude legs, decking, connector hardware, or anchoring — Bestmade specifically advertises all-in per-section pricing because dealers so often quote the frame alone.

Also check MN DNR shoreline rules for your lake before finalizing length; seasonal docks within DNR size limits generally don't need a permit, but local lake associations sometimes have their own restrictions.
